Undirect

A Safari extension for Mac and iPhone that stops a page taking your click. It clears the invisible layers that catch a press meant for something else, refuses windows the site itself never asked to open, and keeps a list of every place a page tried to send you.

The Idea

Pop networks do not wait for anyone to click their ad. They lay a transparent screen over the page and take the next press, or they build a link and click it themselves, or they open a blank tab and rewrite the address of the one you were reading.

I read the code of two of these networks before writing a line of my own. Their own function names describe the trick: a full-viewport form with no colour, an anchor clicked from inside their handler, a check for whether you pressed something that was not a link.

How It Works

The guard runs in two places at once. One half sits beside the page and clears the click catchers while your cursor is still moving, so the press that follows lands where you aimed it. The other half runs in the page's own world, where a pop can call for a new window, and refuses any window whose destination belongs to neither the site nor its own code.

Anything it catches is named, and the name becomes a blocking rule. The second attempt dies as a network request rather than as a fight in JavaScript.

What It Catches

An invisible form laid over the whole page. A transparent layer at the top of the stacking order. A link the page clicked without you. A link rewritten between your press and your release, which is put back so you still arrive where you meant to go. A window opened by code that does not belong to the site.

You Decide

Every cross-site destination gets a row, whether or not it was stopped, so nothing happens out of sight. Each one can be allowed or blocked for that site alone or for everywhere, and the default for anything unknown is yours to set.

Where a site already offers a way to turn its ads off, the extension uses that instead of fighting the page.

Privacy

Nothing leaves the device. There is no account, no analytics, and no server. The list of destinations lives in the browser, and the app reads it through a shared container on the same machine.

Built With

Swift and SwiftUI for the app on both platforms, and a Safari web extension in plain JavaScript with no dependencies. Blocking uses Safari's declarative rules, so the extension never sees the traffic it stops.

The detectors are covered by a browser harness that reproduces each trick and checks the guard undoes it.

Availability

Built for macOS and iOS and prepared for the App Store. The source is public.
